I am an Associate Professor in the Faculty of Geographical Science at Beijing Normal University. My work focuses on land ice dynamics in the climate system, especially the physical mechanisms that connect glaciers and ice sheets with the atmosphere, ocean, sea level, water resources, and cryospheric hazards.

My research combines physics-based ice-flow modeling, Earth system science, remote sensing, and emerging artificial intelligence methods. I develop and apply land ice models for the Antarctic and Greenland ice sheets, High Mountain Asia, and mountain glaciers on the Tibetan Plateau. I have led the development of the two-dimensional Polythermal Land Ice Model (PoLIM) and the three-dimensional Polar Land Ice Simulator (PoLarIS), and have also contributed to the U.S. Department of Energy MPAS-Albany Land Ice Model (MALI).

I participated throughout the Antarctic ice-sheet model intercomparison work supporting IPCC AR6, including initMIP-Antarctica, LARMIP-2, ABUMIP, and ISMIP6, and I am currently involved in the ISMIP7 effort for IPCC AR7. My field experience spans Antarctica, the Arctic, Greenland, the Tibetan Plateau, Xinjiang, and the Qilian Mountains.

Current themes include:

* Antarctic and Greenland ice-sheet instability and future sea-level rise
* Glacier change, runoff timing, and water resources in High Mountain Asia
* Glacier detachment, ice collapse, and cryospheric hazard modeling
* Physically grounded ice-flow models integrated with AI and observations
* Open, China-developed land ice modeling capability through PoLIM and PoLarIS
